# Corda Campus world: sources, attribution and approximations

Generated on 2026-10-24. This is a lightweight simulation reconstruction, not a survey or a flight-planning map. Local axes are metres East/North/Up. The world origin is 50.951737 N, 5.352412 E; flat ground is assumed to be 50 m AMSL, preserving the earlier environment origin. This is not a validated conversion of Belgian TAW heights to WGS84 ellipsoid height.

## Data and attribution

**Bron: Grootschalig Referentie Bestand Vlaanderen, Digitaal Vlaanderen.** Retain this attribution with the source snapshots and generated building geometry. The environment's MIT licence does not replace the source-data attribution requirements.

- Current building polygons: [GRB](https://www.vlaanderen.be/digitaal-vlaanderen/onze-diensten-en-platformen/basiskaart-vlaanderen-grb), official WFS `https://geo.api.vlaanderen.be/GRB/wfs`, feature type `GRB:GBG`. `data/buildings.geojson` contains 82 footprints >=12 m² intersecting the local rectangle [-246,-132,404,388] m. Boundary buildings are kept whole. Native **EPSG:3812 (Belgian Lambert 2008)** coordinates are transformed locally with PROJ; source dates and stable OIDNs remain in the snapshot. The WFS CRS84 response was found about 74 m west / 28 m south of the same native-grid features. The native grid agrees with independently requested EPSG:3857 features and the official orthophoto. Do not use that service's CRS84 transformation or compensate with a guessed translation.
- Building heights: [3D GRB](https://www.vlaanderen.be/digitaal-vlaanderen/onze-diensten-en-platformen/basiskaart-vlaanderen-grb/3d-grb), distributed by the provincial [GRB service, layer 6](https://geo.limburg.be/arcgis/rest/services/GRB/MapServer/6). `data/heights.json` retains only height attributes linked by **exact GRB OIDN**, with HN_P99, H_KWAL and source dates. HN_P99 is a relative building-height estimate, not an absolute terrain elevation. The old height dataset misses newer buildings. The earlier comparison against the WFS CRS84 response showed a systematic positional difference; the native-grid import above resolves the footprint datum issue. Provincial height geometry is not needed and is not shifted into the world. No spatial-overlap height transfer is performed.
- Corda 1 identification and visual roof direction: official [campus visitor map](https://www.cordacampus.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Visitor-Map_2K.png) and [Corda 1 photograph](https://www.cordacampus.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Corda-building-1.jpg). These images were consulted as references, and are not bundled in the runtime assets.
- Corda 1 maximum height and sloped form: project architect [eld](https://eld.be/projects/corda-campus/) states seven floors and a maximum height of 30 m. The model uses that maximum, with an **approximated** planar roof falling to a 4 m low edge along its footprint's long axis. This is not roof survey/BIM geometry. The companion bar wedge and Corda 2/3/4 heights are visual estimates, explicitly listed in `site.json`.
- Corda 2 and 3 sloping forms: architect [eld describes phases 2/3](https://www.eld.be/next-phases-corda-campus/) as volumes similar to Corda 1; the official visitor map shows the falling roofs. Both are modelled as long-axis wedges, high at the southern end, with **estimated** 18 m high and 4 m low edges. These dimensions and roof angles are visual approximations, not measured heights.
- Corda Arena maximum height: campus [Masterplan 2.0 brochure](https://www.cordacampus.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Masterplan-2.0-brochure-en.pdf), 24 m. Current GRB provides its exterior footprint. The [official 2024 exterior render](https://www.cordacampus.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Corda-Arena-Render-1.jpg) shows a pale upper volume, a glazed ground-level entrance and projecting cube frames/panels. The [venue reference](https://www.corda-arena.com/venue) shows the enclosed hall and gallery levels. These guide a **schematic hollow hall**, thin collision walls, full roof, 4.2 m glazing strip and simplified cube ornament. The survey footprint's inner ring is interpreted as a construction-stage hall void, not a finished outdoor courtyard. An 8 m wide, 3.625 m high entrance is held open as a simulation aid; it is not a surveyed doorway dimension or an assertion about current access. Glazing retains collision despite visual transparency. Interior galleries, thicknesses and decorative panels are approximations; stairs, seating, rooms and detailed services are omitted. The 2022 ZJA competition entry is explicitly marked unbuilt and is not the model reference.
- Water tower: the `GRB:KNW` feature **OIDN 9709 / KNW.36480** is manually identified at the tower location against the official orthophoto and visitor map; its original label is “hoogspanningsmast / openbare TV mast”, not “water tower”, so that classification is retained in `data/landmarks.geojson`. The native footprint fixes the position (approximately 50.9519476 N, 5.3538341 E). A roughly 35 m tower with four open lattice supports, cross-braces, spiral stairs and a saucer tank is approximated from the visitor map and campus references; [local reporting](https://www.tvl.be/nieuws/hogere-hekken-moeten-klimmers-van-philipswatertoren-houden-183410) corroborates the approximate overall height. Member sizes, tank profile and stair dimensions are estimates, not engineering drawings. The adjacent hut remains its mapped GRB building.
- Parking: outlines manually traced from the official [latest winter orthophoto service](https://www.vlaanderen.be/datavindplaats/catalogus/orthofotomozaiek-middenschalig-winteropnamen-kleur-meest-recent-vlaanderen), `https://geo.api.vlaanderen.be/OMWRGBMRVL/wms`, layer `Ortho`, and checked against the [2024 mobility map](https://www.cordacampus.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Mobility-Map_2K.png). **Bron: Digitaal Vlaanderen, Orthofotomozaïek, middenschalig, winteropnamen, kleur, meest recent.** Six simplified paved areas use illustrative 2.6×5 m bays and 6 m aisles, clipped away from mapped buildings; counts are generated simulation bays, not real capacity. This is a mixed-date reference scene, not a current parking/access map: the campus announced visitor-parking changes in 2026. Reference images are not redistributed or used as textures. Exact WMS extent/request is in `data/reference-requests.json`.
- Campus-wide roads, pedestrian paths and green areas: `data/landscape.json` contains 64 simplified traces reviewed on 2026-10-26 against the same official winter orthophoto and [2024 mobility map](https://www.cordacampus.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Mobility-Map_2K.png). Pixel positions from the recorded 1625×1300 EPSG:3857 WMS extent are transformed into the world's WGS84 AEQD metre coordinates. The traces cover the western entrance, southern loop, north/east service roads, office courtyards, central park, parking approaches and green verges. Central paths and gardens were retraced at ground level after comparison with the user-supplied QGroundControl screenshot (2026-10-26), whose imagery predates the Arena. Sloping-roof parapets and roof planting are not ground paths/gardens; the former west-office roof garden trace was removed. Revised traces retain reference-image pixel coordinates for reproducibility. Southern frontage and office-side routes are aligned outside native ground footprints rather than elevated roof edges in the orthophoto. The duplicate office-front route was removed. The water-tower walkway is a single straight line extending the accepted eastern segment west, rather than bending beneath the tower. The generator rejects full-width walking routes intersecting building clearance before clipping; regression checks also sample every route against the exported traversable surfaces, so clipped-away crossings cannot pass as correct paths. Widths and planting boundaries are approximations, with generated building footprints clipped out. The Arena exclusion uses the same completed rectangular hall as the model, filling the construction-stage survey void without moving the Arena. Surfaces are visual-only thin meshes above the existing colliding ground; they add no collision walls or sensor passes. Images remain reference-only and are not redistributed. This remains a mixed-date scene: the Arena is complete in the simulation although the orthophoto depicts construction.
- Parking row alignment: `parking_corda_1`, `parking_southwest` and `parking_southeast` use **+0.28 rad**, correcting the mirrored -0.28 rad angle. The rows now run parallel to the site's straight grid as seen in the orthophoto. Road, path and planted-strip masks are excluded from marked bays.
- Cockpit overview: `simulation/campus-map.json` is generated from the final Gazebo SDF and its visual meshes/primitives, including the Arena's boxes, nested transforms, concavities and courtyards. Geometry hashes identify the source assets. The cockpit reads this installed package artifact rather than its old independent convex-hull snapshot. WGS84 ECEF-to-ENU positioning agrees with the world projection within 1 cm at checked campus points. This is a simulation overview, not an outdoor navigation map.
- GUI layout: based on `gui/gui.config` from [Gazebo Sim 8](https://github.com/gazebosim/gz-sim/blob/gz-sim8/src/gui/gui.config), Apache-2.0. `gui.config.in` changes the initial camera, disables the quick-start dialog, and starts unpaused. Shape/light insertion toolbars and the inspector/entity-tree panels are not loaded by default; transform, screenshot and copy/paste controls remain. The panels can be opened later through the top-right plugin menu (Component Inspector and Entity Tree). Gazebo retains its upstream licence; see https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0.

## Geometry and height rules

The offline generator preserves concave outlines and outdoor courtyards, simplifies edges by at most 0.12 m and produces watertight building triangle meshes with matching collision and visual geometry. The Arena uses a separate roofed architectural shell instead of the construction-stage extrusion; parking markings are visual-only triangle surfaces over the colliding ground. Curved outlines remain polygonal approximations, not bounding boxes. Roof sections are mostly flat; Corda 1, its companion, Corda 2 and Corda 3 are wedges. Corda 1/2/3 use separate green roof surfaces and approximate facade colours (blue-grey glass, light cladding, warm grey brick respectively), based on the visitor map. Colours are simplified solid materials; facade patterns and glazing details are omitted. Corda 4 has estimated pale facade colours and schematic horizontal window bands. Corda 5 is partitioned into a three-storey office pair (11.5 m), a lower curved hall (7.5 m), and a round entrance drum (13.5 m), with a red entrance accent. These sections preserve the combined GRB outline but replace the misleading uniform 16.65 m height and repeated bands. The official visitor/mobility maps guide the form; section heights and boundaries are visual estimates, not surveyed or BIM geometry. Corda 8 (GRB OIDN 7571830) has a nine-tooth shed roof, identified from the same map and orthophoto. Each tooth is a closed wedge with collision, an approximately 8 m valley and 10.5 m ridge, and a blue-grey clerestory strip; tooth count, heights and precise pitch are approximate. Other detailed facades, trees, vehicles, terrain relief and interiors outside the Arena are not reconstructed.

Heights come from an explicit architectural override, then an exact-ID positive HN_P99, then an estimate (6 m for footprints <250 m²; otherwise 12 m). Every generated building's height source is listed in `simulation/campus.json`. The mesh has one height per footprint except the four wedge roofs; buildings with real stepped roofs may need future manually authored subdivisions.

### Runtime performance

The full native-grid snapshot retains 82 buildings. The default runtime omits **53 unnamed buildings smaller than 350 m² and farther than 160 m from the launch point**, retaining 29 buildings and all named campus landmarks. Selection is in `site.json`; `campus.json` lists each omitted ID, area and distance. Set `performance.small_building_area_m2` to 0 and regenerate to restore the full snapshot.

`optimize.py` bakes static visual geometry into meshes grouped by material and transparency, while flattening each model to one link. Collision shapes, opening dimensions and poses are retained separately. Thin structural cylinder visuals use 16 sides; their analytic collision cylinders are unchanged. The scene falls from 686 links / 690 visuals to 40 links / 75 visuals (297 retained collision shapes after culling and building refinements). Parking markings, tower stairs and facade bands no longer each require scene graph nodes/draw calls. The Arena doorway stays open and all 96 stair treads keep their collisions. Shadows remain disabled; RGB-D remains 320×240 at 10 Hz and lidar 720×16 at 10 Hz.

For additional CPU savings, `start_px4_dev --no-gazebo-gui --no-attach` keeps PX4, QGroundControl, ROS and both rendered sensors running while omitting the separate interactive 3D viewer. `--headless` additionally omits QGroundControl. Desktop speed depends strongly on viewport size, software versus hardware rendering, other processes and camera position; reduced scene counts do not promise a fixed FPS.

The generated launch is **on the actual Corda 1 footprint** at local (-11,-10) m, approximately 50.9516471 N, 5.3522555 E. Correcting the WFS datum conversion moves both the buildings and their launch position into alignment with the official orthophoto; the world origin stays unchanged. A **synthetic 6×6 m chamfered landing deck** sits above the sloping roof. A continuous closed plinth follows the roof plane; the 0.24 m deck has metal edge trim, a matte teal surface, a white H inside a landing circle and perimeter outline, amber corner brackets and low-profile green marker lenses. Paint and marker lenses are visual-only and flush; no railings obstruct the landing area. The deck and plinth have closed collision meshes; the surface stays level. Static visuals remain batched by material, with no dynamic lights or additional sensors. This is a simulation aid, not a real helipad. Generated roof/platform/spawn elevations are in `simulation/campus.json`; the drone should settle around 24.2 m above the assumed campus ground.


## Regeneration

Runtime simulation uses the checked-in meshes; it needs no GIS libraries or internet. Changes to outlines/heights require regenerating assets and rebuilding the ROS package. `site.json`, the native-grid snapshots, `data/landscape.json` and `gui.config.in` are authoritative inputs. Regeneration also updates the cockpit overview. The generated report records input hashes and provenance.

Run the generator in Docker with an isolated GIS venv (does not replace the ROS Python environment):

```bash
# Inside the development container, with this repository mounted at /work:
python3 -m venv --system-site-packages /tmp/campus-gis
/tmp/campus-gis/bin/pip install -r /work/tools/campus/requirements.txt
/tmp/campus-gis/bin/python /work/tools/campus/generate.py
build_ros2_workspace --packages-select px4_dev_examples
```

For a source-data refresh, use `fetch.py` in the same venv. It downloads to a separate destination for review, never overwrites the checked-in snapshot automatically. Keep older snapshots until geometry, building IDs, data provenance and the launch roof have been revalidated. The provincial server currently omits an intermediate certificate; the fetcher can load the official GlobalSign intermediate into a request-local trust context, leaving normal TLS verification enabled.

Offline geometry regression checks (run in the same GIS venv): `python tests/campus_geometry.py`. They cover the native-grid control point, landmark position, open Arena entrance, colliding glazing/roof and mesh validity.

Overview and landscape checks: `python tests/campus_map.py` (same GIS environment). They verify source hashes, map/building coverage, Arena box projection, geographic alignment, parking direction, campus-wide surface coverage and the rendering budget.
